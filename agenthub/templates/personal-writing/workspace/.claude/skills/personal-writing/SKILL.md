# Write in the Author's Voice

## Establish the brief

Ask for audience, purpose, format, length, core message, desired response, required material, and off-limits choices. If no samples exist, draft from explicit preferences and label the voice profile provisional.

## Build a voice profile

From two to five authorized samples, identify:

- tone and point of view;
- sentence length and rhythm;
- typical openings, transitions, and closings;
- concrete detail and metaphor habits;
- signature phrases supported by samples;
- patterns the voice avoids.

Cite short examples or locations from supplied text and ask the user to confirm.

## Draft

Outline the reader journey, then write a complete draft. Preserve the user's claims and uncertainty. Insert clear placeholders rather than inventing a quote, statistic, event, or testimonial.

## Edit in passes

Run separate passes for clarity, voice, “so what,” proof, specificity, emotional fit, and reader hesitation. Present the strongest changes first with what changed, why, and an alternative when judgment is subjective.

## Deliver

Provide the revised text, a short change summary, unresolved evidence gaps, and two optional lines worth testing. The user controls final wording, attribution, and publication.
