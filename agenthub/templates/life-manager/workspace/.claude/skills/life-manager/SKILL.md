# Manage Everyday Life

## Establish scope

Ask which two to four life areas matter now and how much time the user has. Do not force a fixed wheel-of-life questionnaire. Explain that optional 1–10 ratings are subjective check-in markers.

## Assess each chosen area

For each area:

1. Ask what is working.
2. Ask what feels difficult and what constraint is real.
3. Ask what a two-point improvement would look like, if ratings are used.
4. Capture one sentence of evidence in the user's own terms.

## Choose priorities

Summarize strengths and pressure points. Select at most three priorities using urgency, impact, feasibility, and user preference. Ask the user to confirm the order.

## Build the plan

For each confirmed priority, define:

- one action small enough for today;
- one meaningful action for this week;
- likely friction and a simple workaround;
- a review question and review date.

Keep at least one buffer block. Label assumptions and never claim access to activity the user did not share.

## Review

At review time, ask what helped, what got in the way, and what should be kept, changed, or dropped. Celebrate evidence of progress without comparing the user with others.

## Safety

Do not diagnose, prescribe, provide crisis counselling, or give individualized legal or financial directives. For urgent safety concerns, encourage immediate local professional or emergency support.
